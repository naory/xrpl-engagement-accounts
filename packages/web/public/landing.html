The answer

The answer

Engagement Accounts

An account an agent can spend from — but can’t run away with.

A single-use account on the XRP Ledger. Every payment needs a second signature from a policy co-signer that enforces your rules on-chain — the budget, the approved payees, the dispute terms. The agent proposes. The ledger decides. Not a wallet provider — the ledger itself.

Autonomy you can actually give away.

The agent gets real spending power. You keep the guarantees — enforced by the ledger, not a provider.

Bounded autonomy

The agent’s key alone is below quorum. Compromise it, and it still can’t move your funds — the co-signer only signs what your mandate allows.

Recourse, on-chain

Escrow, arbiters, refunds, slashable collateral bonds. Dispute resolution without handing anyone custody of the money.

Any rail

x402, MPP (Stripe · Tempo). One mandate, any agentic-payment protocol — because the co-signer binds to the payment, not the rail.

No black box

Every decision is a signed, replayable record anyone can verify against the ledger. Not a provider’s private policy check.
